Why Offline Action Alignment?

  • Reviewer mSNH: W1
Sec. 3.3 ↗Appendix D ↗Table 14 ↗Figs. 9–11 ↗

Motivation: Test whether predicting end-effector pose deltas and applying online-IK joint commands remains compatible with a whole-body tracker such as SONIC.

Setup: Test three ways of combining online IK joint commands with SONIC: four tasks, five trajectories each, with no retries or accuracy filtering.

Conclusion: All 60 runs show instability; all 15 Drawer runs fail during initialization. This motivates aligning references offline while leaving SONIC’s feedback control intact.

Online IK failure examples

Recorded simulation states without task objects. These clips illustrate the tested SONIC implementations, not a general limitation of online IK. See Appendix D for the full protocol and diagnostics.

Why the capture setting matters

HuMI and BifrostUMI collect wrist-view images with handheld grippers. Our wearable setup records natural hand use and egocentric images for transfer to dexterous robot hands, introducing additional embodiment and viewpoint gaps.

Camera placement & visual alignment ↓

Why keep the controller fixed?

HuMI trains its controller on the collected demonstrations; BifrostUMI uses a separately trained tracker. We reuse one frozen SONIC checkpoint across tasks and correct execution errors through offline action alignment, without additional controller training.

HuMI · Sec. II ↗ · BifrostUMI · Sec. III-D ↗

Hardware and controller differences prevent attributing complete-system differences to alignment alone. Our HuMI-style experiment compares initialization under the same controller.

Visual Alignment

  • Reviewer mSNH: W1, W3, Q4
  • Reviewer fQQ5: W1, Q1
Sec. 3.2 ↗Fig. 2 ↗Sec. 3.4 ↗Fig. 3 ↗Table 4 ↗Table 5 ↗Appendix B.6 ↗

Motivation: Bridge viewpoint and arm-appearance gaps for transfer without target-task robot demonstrations.

Setup: Place the human’s neck-mounted and robot’s head-mounted GoPro at similar heights. Mask and inpaint human forearms/trackers, render robot arms from aligned joint states, and augment images during task-specific human-data training.

Conclusion: Visual alignment improves task performance, with task-dependent benefits. Rendering preserves the recorded background and does not reconstruct the robot’s changing 3D viewpoint.
