Coordinated Whole-body Motion Transfer

  • Reviewer mSNH: W1
Sec. 3.3 ↗Appendix D ↗Table 14 ↗Figs. 9–11 ↗

Motivation: Transfer coordinated whole-body motion from human demonstrations. We replace EgoHumanoid’s decoupled controller with SONIC’s whole-body tracker.

Setup: This controller change creates a new alignment problem: applying online-IK joint commands from predicted end-effector pose deltas can interfere with SONIC’s balance feedback. The tested variants below show this instability.

Conclusion: We therefore align motion references offline, allowing SONIC to execute them through its own feedback control.

Online IK failure examples

Visual transfer: Similar camera heights and image augmentation reduce viewpoint gaps; robot-arm rendering additionally addresses appearance differences.

Hardware & Capture Setting

  • Reviewer mSNH: W1
  • Reviewer fQQ5: W1, Q1
Sec. 3.2 ↗Fig. 2 ↗

Motivation: Support scalable, dexterous human-to-humanoid loco-manipulation.

Setup: HuMI/BifrostUMI use handheld grippers and wrist views. Our setup differs in two ways.

Human hands → dexterous robot hands. Avoiding matched gripper hardware offers potential for lower hardware overhead, scalable capture and more dexterous tasks. It also requires more demanding action alignment.

Egocentric vision. Scene context supports loco-manipulation, such as Pull Curtain, but requires additional image alignment.

Conclusion: These opportunities introduce alignment challenges beyond the gripper-based setting; fair system comparisons must account for these differences.

Alignment with a General Controller

  • Reviewer mSNH: W1
Sec. 3.3 ↗Table 1 ↗

Motivation: Improve endpoint tracking for manipulation without retraining the whole-body controller.

Setup: HuMI trains its controller on collected demonstrations; BifrostUMI uses a separately trained tracker. We align actions to the same frozen, open-source SONIC checkpoint across tasks.

Conclusion: Offline alignment compensates for tracking errors while retaining a shared controller. System comparisons must account for this difference in controller adaptation.
