03 / NEW SIMULATION DIAGNOSTIC

Motion under external resistance.

mSNH Q3 · vfta Q2

Fixed aligned motion tokens and the same SONIC controller are replayed with opposing forces from 0 to 20 N. Choose a task and a force level to compare against the unloaded motion.

Full-sequence position error (cm)

All 21 force levels ↓
Experiment scope

One trajectory per task and one replay per force level, without re-optimization. Resistance acts opposite the instantaneous velocity at the operating hand or foot during a predefined interaction window. The red arrow shows the applied force. The 0–20 N values are test settings, not measured object resistance.

Errors are full-sequence bilateral means in the paper’s local coordinate convention, including unloaded frames and the non-operating limb. This tests resistance sensitivity in simulation; it does not model grasp formation, static friction, slippage or object mechanics. The drawer uses the released aligned sample.

04 / NEW SIMULATION DIAGNOSTIC

What changes when the image stops changing?

fQQ5 Q3

Compare time-varying recorded images with a fixed first frame, black image, fixed noise or white image. Each video shows the simulated motion and the image actually supplied to the policy.

Read as a qualitative diagnostic. Fixed-first-frame rollouts show limited action progression in these examples. The comparison concerns the supplied visual sequence; the recorded-video condition is not an online camera responding to the simulated robot.
What is held fixed?

Each task uses the same policy checkpoint, initial pose and action-sampling noise across conditions, with simulated body-state feedback. The policy predicts a 100-frame action chunk at 50 Hz; simulation pauses during inference. Noise is a single fixed RGB image. There is one example per task; these clips are not task-success measurements. The first three tasks use the available bs256 policies and raw human recordings; the drawer uses its bs32 policy.

Less collection labor.

On Close Laptop and Pull Curtain, 200 human demonstrations achieve a mean task score close to 50 robot demonstrations (53.33% vs. 60.00%), with the same observed full-task success: 40% and 35%. This is a labor-efficiency advantage, not equal demonstration-count efficiency.

06 / SELECTION & PROCESSING

What is kept, rejected, and spent?

Table 11 · Appendix A.4 ↗

Historical candidates are ranked by task-dependent tracking and motion metrics. A demonstration is rejected if no candidate satisfies the pelvis-displacement criterion. The best 200 accepted demonstrations per task are retained for training.

Percentages use all inputs as the denominator. Mean D round includes only demonstrations assigned to D. Time is elapsed processing time for the entire task input set on four workstations in parallel, including inputs ultimately rejected. Average weights tasks equally.

Automated selection. Candidate scoring reuses the existing rollout measurements and requires no manual review. Its numerical calculations add negligible overhead relative to alignment. The reported collection-labor saving is distinct from offline compute cost.

Light preprocessing

Before VLA training, hand commands for the fridge and curtain tasks are binarized, and trajectories are lightly filtered. Incidental demonstrator movement at the end of curtain sequences is trimmed.

These are data-preparation steps; this revision does not add a separate rule-removal ablation.

08 / INTERPRETATION

What the evidence establishes.

mSNH Q4 · fQQ5 Q1

Zero-shot robot transfer

Task-specific policies are trained on aligned human demonstrations and deployed without target-task robot demonstrations. The 50-task simulation study evaluates motion alignment; it is not a demonstration of a single policy’s unseen-task generalization.

Image–action consistency

Rendering adapts arm appearance while preserving the recorded background. Image-space alignment does not reconstruct the robot’s changing 3D viewpoint. The visual ablation measures the combined benefit of rendering and augmentation; it does not isolate a residual 3D consistency error.

Comparison scope

The distinctive contribution is offline execution-aware refinement under a fixed whole-body controller. The HuMI-style experiment compares motion initialization under the same controller, rather than complete end-to-end systems.
